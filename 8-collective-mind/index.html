Who Says Individuality Will Stay Forever?

Daniel Burger·19 November 2026·3,294 words

TL;DR

The third and last outcome of my Ontology is called "collective mind": as soon as we've achieved synthetic consciousness via hybrid mind uploading, we'll technically have the possibility to merge into one giant collective mind. Who says that we stay individuals?

Originally posted on XReplies and discussion happen there.Open on X →

The third and last outcome of my Ontology, when working on the hybrid mind uploading mission towards a future state of synthetic consciousness as the vision, is called "collective mind". This is probably THE(!) weirdest item in the entire Ontology, and that's actually a wild thing to say on its own if you consider that we're talking about super weird stuff already, such as hybrid mind uploading, substrate independence, solving death and transcending humanity, etc. The reason being that, based on my own experience of talking to longevity people over the past few years, even the most extreme transhumanists usually have one view in common about the future post-death and post-augmentation: they stay single individuals, "lasting as long as they want/forever". But who says that we stay individuals? Who says that we don't merge into one collective, and also phenomenally conscious, mind? (I mean that last bit literally btw, as a society or an internet can be smart as a whole without there being anything it is like to be it, and here there would be). Most transhumanists that I've talked to (myself included, initially) usually don't consider this a possibility at all, they're all pro-individualism. Last year I suggested using exactly that as a little stress test for people in brain augmentation, so to ask them if they're pro-hivemind or pro-individualism ([Sep 2025]), only to admit three days later that I was losing arguments for individualism ([Sep 2025]). Nowadays I'm pretty unsure about it. Nobody is truly seeing a strong convergence towards collective minds, I'm pretty sure that it might happen, and I might be completely wrong.

It was around late December, after Christmas till early January of this year, when I had, funnily enough, three different contexts "meeting" around me towards discussing hiveminds and collective minds for some reason (on New Year's Eve I even posted a thought experiment about a corpus callosum that gets stretched to a kilometre, until the two hemispheres stop being one whole: [Dec 2025]). It was also during that time that I was writing that essay on the breakdown of the forward arrow of phenomenal time due to engineered, steerable substrates for brains, where I realised (in hindsight it feels obvious) that it's quite possible that as soon as we've achieved synthetic consciousness via hybrid mind uploading, we'll technically have the possibility to merge into one giant collective mind, with one giant process world-line instead of "society". So the things discussed in the first few meditations about the generational, global approach to death and the individual, first-principles one are basically converging towards being the same thing, as we all just merge into one mind. (On day one I kept global death (no conscious observer left anywhere) apart from individual death (THIS consciousness ends), and said that Musk's route of making us fault tolerant as a species answers a different question than mine. With one world-line for everybody, "consciousness goes on" and "this consciousness goes on" would be the same sentence. It would also mean that the redundancy of eight billion separate lines is gone btw, so that one line had better be fault tolerant in itself).

So what's in that essay (a working document from January, that I never published and only circulated around close friends)? I get to hiveminds via an area that has nothing to do with them at first sight, which is memory. If you take Holger Lyre's neurophenomenal structuralism seriously ([academic.oup.com]), then what an experience is like is fixed by its position in a structure of relations to all other experiences (he calls it Q-structure), and HOW that position gets activated, by the eyes or by a memory, shouldn't matter. But it obviously does, as a "remembered sunset" is a pale and somewhat wobbly thing next to the one that I'm standing in front of. The answer in my essay from January was that this comes from how a biological brain stores things (things being engrams => physical traces/manifestations of information/memory, etc.) => it keeps a compressed, lossy version of it and rebuilds the rest from priors when I remember (I called that "ecphoric reconstruction"), so remembering lands somewhere near the original position and never exactly on the original's phenomenal position. Take that bottleneck away on a substrate that we built without this compression constraint, and a memory would be the full experience again (=> if that can be built at all; we don't know. I called it "lossless Q-storage"). A past that I can re-enter at full (phenomenal) resolution isn't "gone" in the way that we're usually/naturally used to. Personal time turns from something that you get "pushed through" (forward in time) into something that I can "walk around in", while the substrate itself keeps "moving forward" like everything else in ordinary physical reality (think of a DVD player => you can rewind the film as often as you like, the player doesn't get a day younger. I'm btw not implying some form of duality here, both things are still within physics and not some meta-reality, but obviously the phenomenal reality can now, without compression, be bidirectional). I called these two layers Q-time and substrate time (P-time from here on, for physical) – and the "destruction of the forward arrow" in the title of the essay from January only ever happens in the first one: Q-time (again, not implying any property dualism here). And if one can go back into a past experiential state, then one can also e.g. decide differently from there on and see "where it goes" => so the history of what one has experienced stops being a simple line and rather turns into a graph with branches (a directed acyclic graph, an idea/picture that I borrowed from Toby Ord).

Btw, quite importantly, there are two kinds of "hivemind" hiding in this conversation so far: The first is the Q-time kind => it's intrapersonal, meaning just one substrate and just one ongoing process => and many of "me" in the sense of all the branches that I've walked through and can walk into again (that's the "branching identity" and the "directed acyclic graph of selves"). Nobody else is involved and in P-time there's still exactly one of me. The second is the P-time kind, which is interpersonal: several people in one phenomenal space at the same physical time (the "collective convergence"). My January essay only had the first kind written out. The second was in there as two half-sentences that I actually left as open questions => that branches might be merged again (fusion, which raises its own complexities, etc.), and that one person's graph might be spread over several substrates or substrate classes. Now read those two together => as soon as we spread over substrates and start to share phenomenal objects between each other, we're just a few iterations away from merging into one thing, and from the inside that thing wouldn't even be a line any more, as it'd have everybody's branches in it. One world-line in P-time (its substrate still gets "older" like everything else), and in Q-time one non-linear thing that doesn't point forward! This might be one of the wildest outcomes of all of this. I need the technical side of it anyway btw, as one of the operational requirements in the Ontology is called "distributed unification" (many nodes, one mind) => a brain that gets replaced piece by piece is for a long time a mix of old tissue and new parts, and that mix has to be ONE mind the whole time and not two. If that can be done between the parts of one brain, then I don't see what in the method would care about whose skull the parts came from. That's why this is an outcome in the Ontology and no goal (a goal we strive for and could fail at, an outcome follows either way, day four). What follows either way is only that it becomes possible (same as on day five) – and that we'd actually end up there is my gut feeling.

There are also other indicators, if you think about it: The internet and then the world wide web are things that interconnect us already, globalisation too, and some people in the BCI space already talk about inter-personal BCIs (in 2019 three people played a kind of Tetris together, two of them sending a yes or a no each out of their EEG and the third one getting it through a magnetic coil on the head, at about 81% accuracy: [nature.com]). Now, when ultimately the substrate becomes the BCI and we have steerable phenomenology, then ultimately we can also just merge. It seems like initially people will most likely do it occasionally, and for a pretty boring reason, which is to SHOW something instead of telling it. Take something as dull as how I multiply in my head. Whatever happens in my phenomenal space when I do 17 × 24, so how it's computed and visualised in there, is most likely completely different from what happens in yours, although we both end up at 408. If I tried to imagine what it's like when YOU do it, I'd use my own way of doing it and assume that yours is roughly the same, and I'd prob be wrong without ever noticing. Feynman has a story about exactly this => he found out that he could read while counting seconds in his head, but not talk. John Tukey didn't believe him, as he himself could talk while counting but not read, and it turned out that Feynman was counting by talking to himself and Tukey by watching a tape with numbers go by. The same word "counting" for both, and they only found out because one of them didn't believe the other (and by trying it out, nobody could have TOLD them). I'd like to clarify that "computed and visualised" is itself just a thing in word form, and that even a much better description would fail to carry over what it's like! That's the "show, don't tell" issue that I connect with Wittgenstein. In the Tractatus he has the line that what can be shown can't be said, and later, in the Investigations, the box with a beetle in it that everybody has and nobody else can look into, where the word "beetle" works just fine whatever is in the box. (A Wittgenstein reader will point out that I'm mixing up what he says about logical form with what he says about private objects, and that's fair). My original essay from January has a more technical version of that box btw, via the principal bundles of Oizumi, Lim and Kanai ([osf.io]) => in my reading, the part of the structure that comes from the symmetries of a sense has to match between two people, while the part where the identities of things sit (they call them signatures) is plastic, with no guaranteed relationship between yours and mine (this does funnily enough also have an impact on the gradual reorganisation principle in [mechanistic] gradual brain replacement).

So what would showing be? If I could let you have what I have while I multiply, with nothing in between that turns it into signals and back, then there'd be nothing left to interpret and no need for an interface, as the "computation" of whatever is happening would be just one stream in one big shared phenomenal space. But then it's also no longer me showing something to you! Husserl saw this almost a hundred years ago (in the fifth Cartesian Meditation) => if what belongs to the other one were directly accessible to me, then it would simply be a part of me, and the two of us would be the same (YES, Husserl being the OG collective mind/hivemind person, who would have thought that!). So whatever sits between two heads and turns things into signals and back is telling again, only with more channels (the Tetris players were telling, one bit at a time), and the moment that something really gets shown, there's a bit less of "two heads" than before. That's why I said that the substrate has to become the interface (i.e. BCI). The closest thing to it that exists, as far as I know, is one pair of twins who are joined at the head with a bridge of tissue between their thalami, and of whom a philosopher has argued that they share some of their experiences and are still two people ([doi.org]).

So it would start as something occasional (two people, one thing to show, and then apart again), and I'd expect more and more of it, as whoever has had something SHOWN once will find telling pretty poor afterwards. A whole class of disagreements would go away with it, namely the one that comes from not getting what the other one means, and there'd be an understanding that I can only call perfect, as there's nothing left to understand ABOUT each other (whether all disagreement goes away, I don't know, as I e.g. manage to disagree with myself just fine :-)). And at some point this most likely passes a threshold, after which coming apart again is the exception, and we've just merged into one giant collective mind. I'm on pretty thin ice here, as I don't know if that threshold is gradual or sharp. There's one theory that says sharp => Christof Koch has spelt out what integrated information theory predicts if two brains get bridged with more and more connections, which is that at some point both minds are abruptly gone and one new one is there instead (he calls it, as he's a fellow German-speaking person, an "Übermind"). I don't need IIT to be right for any of this btw, and my stretched corpus callosum idea from 31st of December 2025 was the same question from the other end.

Now, when I realised this earlier this year, I was quite uneasy about it. For the 10+ years that I've been on this journey to solve death and to work towards synthetic consciousness via (hybrid) mind uploading, I always thought that I'd be sticking around in the future, for as long as I want. But I never imagined that I would most likely "still die" at some point, due to merging into the collective. It's not really a dying; I'm still around, but I'm as much of an individual in the larger collective as a single neuron is in my 86-billion-neuron brain.

"Well, Daniel", one might say, "that doesn't sound like dying at all; you've just said that you're still around!" And yes, I did. By "still around" I mean that nothing of mine stops, as death is the irreversible end of a conscious process world-line (day three), and here nothing ends => two processes keep going and become one. But the vision from day one was a conjunction: consciousness goes on AND this consciousness goes on, and after a merge I can't say what "this" still points at. And my neuron comparison is nastier than it looks, as a neuron in my brain is alive and does its thing, but (panpsychists aside...) nobody thinks that there's somebody in there, and it gets no vote. If THAT is the right picture, then I'd be around the way my neurons are around, as a working part, and my being conscious as me would be over (which is death by day three after all, only without a corpse). The other picture is that what I experience goes on as a part of what the whole experiences, the way the left and the right half of what I see are made in two different hemispheres and still show up as one view without a seam, only that there'd be no separate me left who has it. I can't tell which of the two it is, and I don't think that anybody can at the moment (Koch's version says the first, and the twins rather suggest that there's a lot of room before it comes to that).

There's one example where this has happened before, and it's us and our history. Life was single cells for most of its time on this planet, and then cells started to stick together (not once, but somewhere between 13 and 25 times independently, depending on how one counts: [frontiersin.org]), and in some of those lines to give up reproducing on their own. Maynard Smith and Szathmáry saw the same pattern in many of the big transitions of evolution, which is that the former individuals can afterwards only reproduce as parts of the new whole. Now follow our own line from there. First many cells form a body, then a brain, and at some point there's consciousness (yes, I'm simplifying for the sake of the length of today's meditation). Then this thing starts to make technology, first a few sticks and stones, then fire, then everything up to modern tech and AI, and with that it enters something like an "evolution escape velocity", as it can now engineer what it used to wait for. Then it solves individual death (via hybrid mind uploading). And then it merges, which would be the same step again one level up, and the last outcome of the vision of synthetic consciousness. Seen like that, it almost looks like another huge goal of life (evolution has no goals, I know, so read it as a pattern that keeps repeating). The last transition on Maynard Smith and Szathmáry's own list is language btw, so the arrival of telling, and it's the one where the individuals stayed individuals. Of course, there's only so much that a comparison with cells can carry, as no cell lost a point of view when it joined a body (as far as we know there was none to lose), and with us there are eight billion of them at stake. But language, to bring it back to Wittgenstein, is just a dimensional reduction of our phenomenal space, and it's not really needed any more after cross-sharing "phenomenality" between mechanistic brains.

This is also one of the things that I meant with the lightbulb and the candles on day three of my meditations: We can't say or predict what's actually going to happen in the future, so what the tail of solving death and of moving to a mechanistic substrate will do to us, and what the upshots will be (nobody who worked on the lightbulb saw that electricity would bring superintelligence/ASI). This hivemindism and the breakdown of the forward arrow of time might be among them, and there might be even weirder consequences that I can't see from here. What I can say is how I'd want to find out, and hopefully it sounds a lot like yesterday by now. Say I merge with somebody for an afternoon and then come back => then I have my own baseline to compare with, and can tell what went missing and what got added. But say there's no coming back. Then the "irreversible" in my definition of death starts to bite, as somebody could of course ask the collective afterwards, "Look, I'm not asking for your theory of personhood, I'm asking a simple question: is Daniel still in there, yes or no?", and it would say yes either way (the same problem as with yesterday's emulation!). So occasionally, first, small, one step at a time and with a way back. I'm still not at peace with this outcome tbh (it's literally one of those things that truly keep me awake at night), but it does make the whole thing bigger than the "me, for as long as I want" that I started out with ten years ago, and, well, it's in the public now!
