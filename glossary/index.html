Glossary

Terms as they are used in Meditations on Synthetic Consciousness and Hybrid Mind Uploading by Daniel Burger. These are working definitions and will change as the series does.

Synthetic consciousness

The vision of this series: consciousness that no longer has to end, starting with the consciousness that exists now. Summarised in 0/30 as "engineered phenomenology on a designed substrate you could gradually become". "Synthetic" says that the result is the real kind of consciousness, reached via a creation process; the series calls a consciousness synthetic once it is sustained by created and engineered parts. It is used differently from "artificial consciousness" and "machine consciousness", which 1/30 discusses.

Discussed in 0/30 The Start, 1/30 Causa Finalis, 2/30 What Could Be Hybrid about Mind Uploading?, 3/30 Solving Death, 4/30 Measurements, Models and Theories, 7/30 Augmented Cognition Needs a Baseline First, 8/30 Who Says Individuality Will Stay Forever?, 9/30 Weak and Strong Multiple Realisability

Hybrid mind uploading (HMU)

The mission of this series and the route to synthetic consciousness. Rather than creating something external to the brain that recreates the brain, it builds something that can be integrated into the brain itself. "Hybrid" means that biological and engineered parts carry one and the same process at the same time: what is hybrid is the thing that carries consciousness, never the consciousness itself. It works by non-destructive co-running and gradual replacement, and is distinguished from classical mind uploading, which treats a copy as survival.

Also: HMU

Classical mind uploading (CMU)

The name this series uses for mind uploading via whole brain emulation: scan a brain in as much detail as possible, store it, and then simulate it in order to emulate consciousness on a different substrate, a computer. Some versions are fully destructive. The series does not treat a separate instance reconstructed this way as the survival of the original consciousness.

Also: CMU

Mind uploading

The broader idea whose original goals were solving death, mind augmentation and enabling a new state of humanity, popularised in the Hans Moravec lineage. The series keeps the term for what it promises and drops its old method, splitting it into classical and hybrid mind uploading.

Discussed in 2/30 What Could Be Hybrid about Mind Uploading?, 4/30 Measurements, Models and Theories, 5/30 Transcending Biology, but not Physics, 8/30 Who Says Individuality Will Stay Forever?, 9/30 Weak and Strong Multiple Realisability

Whole brain emulation (WBE)

The academic name for the scan-and-simulate approach to mind uploading, which 2/30 traces to Randal Koene around 1998. In this series, mind uploading via whole brain emulation is what is called classical mind uploading.

Also: WBE

Discussed in 2/30 What Could Be Hybrid about Mind Uploading?, 4/30 Measurements, Models and Theories, 7/30 Augmented Cognition Needs a Baseline First

Process world-line

The 4D trajectory of the physical-dynamical and organised bounded processes going on in your head that constitute what we call your brain. It is a physical claim about the process that does the carrying, not a report of how continuous it feels, since a copy would feel continuous too. In this series, death is the irreversible end of the process world-line.

Also: world-line

Gradual brain replacement

Swapping the brain's substrate out piece by piece without destroying the physical-dynamical and organised bounded processes, ideally while adding repairability and fault tolerance, so that the process world-line hardens and becomes more resilient. It is the replacement side of hybrid mind uploading.

Also: gradual replacement

Mechanistic gradual brain replacement (MGBR)

Named in 3/30 as the core of hybrid mind uploading, and as step one towards solving death, after functional brain isolation. 0/30 says that a different piece of brain can take over a function, but that a different substrate taking over is still open and is the reason for the replacement work. 3/30 says whether a world-line truly went on via a replacement has to be shown physically.

Also: MGBR

Discussed in 0/30 The Start, 3/30 Solving Death, 5/30 Transcending Biology, but not Physics, 11/30 Does My NCC Look Big in This Substrate?

Serial lesion effect

Cited in 0/30, together with Barrett's (2013) gradual reorganisation principle: a different piece of brain can take over a function. What neither of them gives you, the post says, is a different substrate taking over, which is still open.

Also: SLE

Discussed in 0/30 The Start, 6/30 Computational Vs. Physical Substrate Independence, 9/30 Weak and Strong Multiple Realisability

Gradual reorganisation principle

Barrett's (2013) principle, cited in 0/30 together with the serial lesion effect: a different piece of brain can take over a function. What neither of them gives you, the post says, is a different substrate taking over, which is still open.

Also: gradual reorganization principle, GRP

Discussed in 0/30 The Start, 6/30 Computational Vs. Physical Substrate Independence, 8/30 Who Says Individuality Will Stay Forever?, 9/30 Weak and Strong Multiple Realisability

Solving death

The goal that no one has to end, which is different from halting the natural process of dying. Reversing ageing halts the dying but does not solve death, since you can still get hit by a bus. The goal says "no one has to end", never "no one is allowed to end", because only a continuation worth having counts. In the Ontology it is one of the three hard goals the mission has to achieve; functional brain isolation is step zero towards it, and gradual replacement comes after. Solved means the remaining risk keeps falling fast enough that all the yearly risks add up to a finite total.

Also: solve death

Functional brain isolation

Taking a living brain out of its body and keeping it alive outside of it for as long as possible, the work of Eightsix: early success in rodents, work towards a large animal brain, and ultimately humans. In this series it is step zero on the route to solving death, before gradual replacement.

Also: functionally isolated brain, brain isolation

Discussed in 3/30 Solving Death, 6/30 Computational Vs. Physical Substrate Independence, 11/30 Does My NCC Look Big in This Substrate?

Deprivation view of death

Nagel's view, used in 3/30 as the central reason why death is bad: death is the irreversible end of a conscious process world-line, so everything that would have come after does not happen for the someone that this world-line is, and if that would have been worth having, missing out on it is bad for that someone.

Also: deprivation account, deprivationism

Discussed in 1/30 Causa Finalis, 3/30 Solving Death

Adaptive preferences

Elster's term, also called "sour grapes", used in 3/30 for making your peace with death because, for basically all of human history, there was nothing anybody could do about it. The post says that the wish to solve death can be explained away, but so can everybody else's acceptance, and that explaining away doesn't decide anything.

Also: sour grapes, adaptive preference formation

Discussed in 3/30 Solving Death

Genetic fallacy

Used in 3/30 for treating the causes of wanting to solve death (an upbringing, a teenage bookshelf) as if they showed that the goal itself is bad. The post distinguishes a cause (what made you want it) from a reason (what makes it worth wanting): those stories tell you how he ended up with death as his main topic, but they don't tell you at all if the goal itself is any good, and they also can't tell you that it's bad.

Discussed in 3/30 Solving Death

Death

The irreversible end of a conscious process world-line; seen from the engineering side, the irreversible destruction of the physical and dynamic processes of consciousness. "Irreversible" is meant strictly: the end that cannot be undone by anything that physics allows. Medicine keeps pushing the point where a stop turns into an end further out, so solving death is never about bringing somebody back from an end, only about never letting a stop turn into one.

Also: irreversible end

Discussed in 3/30 Solving Death, 5/30 Transcending Biology, but not Physics, 8/30 Who Says Individuality Will Stay Forever?

Amortality

What you get when the dying is halted (ageing, failing organs): no clock in you runs out by itself anymore. It is step zero, not solving death, since a risk that just stays where it is, like being hit by a bus, still gets everybody in the end, only later.

Also: amortal, stop dying

Discussed in 3/30 Solving Death

Immortality

The state in which death cannot occur at all anymore. In the order "stop dying and achieve amortality, then solve death and seek immortality", it is something one can seek once death is solved, and not part of the goal.

Also: immortal

Discussed in 0/30 The Start, 2/30 What Could Be Hybrid about Mind Uploading?, 3/30 Solving Death

Understand consciousness

The second of the three goals of the Ontology. "Understand" comes in three levels: measuring it, modelling it and explaining it. The goal counts as reached at the second level, a model that can tell you before a replacement step if the experience will change; the third is the horizon, and it may never be reachable by science. It is ordered second because it is what is needed to check that death really got solved: hybrid mind uploading can sidestep a theory of consciousness when building the thing that carries the process, but it cannot sidestep measuring.

Also: understanding consciousness

Discussed in 4/30 Measurements, Models and Theories, 5/30 Transcending Biology, but not Physics, 6/30 Computational Vs. Physical Substrate Independence, 7/30 Augmented Cognition Needs a Baseline First

Measurement theory

The most modest of the three: it only tells you what kind of data count at all (reports, behaviour, neural markers, and so on) and how to read that data as experience; it doesn't say why. In this series, measurement comes first, before a full theory, with the two correcting each other in turns, because what you think consciousness is already shapes what you accept as a measurement of it.

Also: measurement theories

Discussed in 4/30 Measurements, Models and Theories

Model of consciousness

With a model you can already predict which experience goes with which physical state. In this series it is the second of three levels of "understand" (measure, model, explain), and the level at which the goal counts as reached: a model that allows us to measure, predict and steer experience across a replacement step, and that can tell you before a step you cannot undo whether the experience will change.

Also: models of consciousness

Discussed in 4/30 Measurements, Models and Theories

Theory of consciousness

A theory would say what consciousness is and why there is any of it in the first place. 4/30 treats this as the third level of "understand", the horizon, and worries that it may belong with questions science cannot settle, so that a theory of consciousness may always be poetry in Bohr's sense. Hybrid mind uploading does not let its success depend on any single theory of consciousness being the only true one; rival theories become predictions that a measurement can test.

Also: theories of consciousness

Discussed in 0/30 The Start, 4/30 Measurements, Models and Theories, 6/30 Computational Vs. Physical Substrate Independence

Goal vs outcome

A goal is something that we strive for and could fail at; an outcome is what follows either way, as long as we work on the mission. Understanding consciousness is a goal, at least up to the level of measurement, and not a mere outcome, because without a way to tell that experience is still there, and that it's still this experience, we could never know if the mission succeeded. 5/30 applies the distinction to transcending humanity: the capability could become possible while nobody goes beyond a limit, or while the minds that do are all replacements. The goal is that we get there as the ones that we are.

Also: goals and outcomes

Discussed in 4/30 Measurements, Models and Theories, 5/30 Transcending Biology, but not Physics

Consciousness engineering

Engineering with matter that we try to couple and integrate with the biological brain, so that experience extends across both parts ("bridging" phenomenal consciousness), with the aim of ultimately letting it host synthetic consciousness. 4/30 uses this name both for that work and for the Frontiers Research Topic Daniel co-organised, and contrasts it with consciousness science: an engineering mindset rather than a science mindset, though the measurement problem of consciousness may be solved from this work.

Discussed in 4/30 Measurements, Models and Theories

Biomimetic neuromorphics

The slot that whole brain emulation has in classical mind uploading, but in hybrid mind uploading. 4/30 expects measurement theories, and maybe even models of consciousness, to emerge from this work, and treats a good model of consciousness here as useful enough to say that we understand consciousness.

Discussed in 4/30 Measurements, Models and Theories

Sidestepping

What hybrid mind uploading does with a theory of consciousness: it does not need a theory in order to make the work succeed, or to have confidence in it, but it does not ignore theories either. Ignoring them completely would be insanity, as you'd end up assuming one anyway. Sidestepping means hybrid mind uploading doesn't let its success depend on any single theory of consciousness being the only true one; it assumes a few things openly (the process world-line is one, held as a hypothesis), and rival theories become predictions that a measurement can test.

Also: sidestep

Discussed in 4/30 Measurements, Models and Theories

Adversarial collaboration

Named in 4/30 as one of the first signs of a rigorisation of the consciousness sciences: theories with conflicting predictions get tested against each other.

Also: adversarial collaborations

Discussed in 4/30 Measurements, Models and Theories

Inventing Temperature

Hasok Chang's example, used in 4/30: thermometers and fixed scales (Fahrenheit, 1724) were around long before a kinetic theory explained what temperature is (1850s–1870s). You can't calibrate a thermometer without knowing what temperature really is, and you can't know what temperature is without a thermometer, so every round of measuring corrected the concept, and also the other way around. This is the picture for how a measurement theory and a theory of consciousness correct each other in turns.

Also: calibration circle

Discussed in 4/30 Measurements, Models and Theories

Transcend humanity

The third and last of the three goals of the Ontology, and the transformative one, where the thing that changes is us. Until 5/30 it was called "human transcendence". It means going beyond the limits that human biology sets for us, and only those: the limits that physics sets stay where they are. What gets left behind is the species, the kind we were born into with the limits of its biology, not the person who goes beyond them, not the people who would rather stay, and not being humane. It has to stay you, and it does not have to stay human. In terms of the process world-line, it is what lets that same world-line outgrow the limits that it was born with. It comes last because it needs both the engineered carrier that solving death brings and more understanding of consciousness than solving death does.

Also: transcending humanity, human transcendence

Discussed in 4/30 Measurements, Models and Theories, 5/30 Transcending Biology, but not Physics, 6/30 Computational Vs. Physical Substrate Independence, 7/30 Augmented Cognition Needs a Baseline First, 8/30 Who Says Individuality Will Stay Forever?

Limits of biology

The limits that come from the thing that carries us, which came with the biology that we were born into. Death is the one that everybody knows. 5/30 names others: what we can experience (vision is roughly one octave of the electromagnetic spectrum), what we can keep (memory compresses what it stores and rebuilds what it recalls), how much a mind can hold, where a mind can exist, and being one mind, closed off from every other one. A telescope, a notebook or a spacesuit go around a limit and leave it where it was, in the thing that carries the experience. They are distinct from the limits that physics sets, which stay where they are, and from the adumbrations of perception, which hold for any mind.

Also: limits that human biology sets

Discussed in 2/30 What Could Be Hybrid about Mind Uploading?, 5/30 Transcending Biology, but not Physics

Adumbrations

Husserl's term (Ideas I, §§41–44), as read in 5/30: walking around a table, I only ever see it from one side at a time, so the table is always "more" than what is given right now, whereas the seeing itself is immanent and does not come in sides. The table is not a thing-in-itself behind its appearances; its transcendence lies in how it is given, which is never completely. Husserl says this holds for any mind that perceives a thing, even for God, so transcending humanity does not try to get rid of it. What can change is how much of that "more" a mind gets to.

Also: Abschattung

Discussed in 5/30 Transcending Biology, but not Physics

Transhumanism

The position that it is possible and desirable to use technology in order to go beyond the limits of human biology, up to a point where the result may be called posthuman, in the transhumanist sense of a being with capacities far beyond ours (not the "posthumanism" of the humanities). Julian Huxley made the word known in the 1950s, with "man remaining man, but transcending himself", meant for the species as a whole. 5/30 starts with the single someone instead and drops the "remaining man". The goal is not called transhumanism because an -ism is something that you hold, and a goal is something that you reach, or don't.

Also: posthuman

Discussed in 5/30 Transcending Biology, but not Physics, 7/30 Augmented Cognition Needs a Baseline First, 8/30 Who Says Individuality Will Stay Forever?

Steerable phenomenology

A carrier that gets built, rather than born, can be built differently: a built substrate is a steerable one. With every gradual replacement step there is a bit of the carrier that does not have to repeat every limit of the tissue that it replaced, and where there is freedom like this the substrate becomes steerable, and with it, bit by bit, the phenomenology. Earlier described as the thing that the transhumanist has long dreamt of without once having thought it.

Also: steerable substrate, built substrate

Discussed in 5/30 Transcending Biology, but not Physics, 7/30 Augmented Cognition Needs a Baseline First, 8/30 Who Says Individuality Will Stay Forever?

Lossless Q-storage

From an unpublished working document of January 2026, "Synthetic Substrates and the Destruction of the Forward Arrow of Personal Time", which leans on Lyre's neurophenomenal structuralism. The "Q" stands for what an experience is like. The thesis is that the losses of memory are a contingent limit of biological storage, not a principled one: a substrate that stored an experience whole would give a memory as rich as the experience and the same at every recall, a personal time one can traverse. There is no guarantee that it can be built, and it involves no time travel, since only the playback changes and the substrate still runs forward. In the Ontology it sits under augmented cognition.

Also: forward arrow of personal time

Discussed in 5/30 Transcending Biology, but not Physics, 8/30 Who Says Individuality Will Stay Forever?, 11/30 Does My NCC Look Big in This Substrate?

World-line fidelity

5/30 states the Ontology's rule: the process world-line never breaks, and nothing gets traded against it, enhancement included. A great deal may change, but there cannot be a moment at which one stops and another takes over. At every step it has to be still you, the "continuer", not a promise that you stay the way you are, or about where you end up. That the world-line really goes on through such changes is a hypothesis.

Also: continuity over similarity

Discussed in 0/30 The Start, 5/30 Transcending Biology, but not Physics, 10/30 I Care Deeply about the Continuity of the Human Brain

Substrate independence

The first of the three outcomes of the Ontology, listed as a state: mind no longer tied to biological substrate. It pairs with the goal of solving death: gradual replacement brings a carrier that is no longer the biology that we were born with. It is an outcome, not a goal, as it comes along with the work, and it was never meant as a guarantee, since how far it can go is decided by physics. 6/30 rejects the reading in which the substrate simply does not matter and keeps only the two words: what it means by them is the re-creation of the brain's constraints in another material, which it calls physical substrate independence.

Also: substrate-independence

Computational substrate independence

What Bostrom and classical mind uploading mean by substrate independence: a mind depends only on the computation, so whatever runs it will do. Bostrom wrote "substrate-independence" into his 2003 simulation argument, introducing it as "a common assumption in the philosophy of mind". 6/30 recounts how, in May 2026, the series filed it under the commitments that fix the answers in advance.

Discussed in 6/30 Computational Vs. Physical Substrate Independence

Physical substrate independence

What the series means by substrate independence: a mind depends on what its carrier physically does, so whatever does the same will do, be it biological or not. What it is supposed to become independent of is biology, and physics it never leaves. It claims less than the computational version, as a material that does the same physics also runs the same computation along the way, but not the other way round. Whether any material other than living cells can do the job is a question for experiments.

Discussed in 6/30 Computational Vs. Physical Substrate Independence, 7/30 Augmented Cognition Needs a Baseline First

Multiple realisability

Putnam's argument in "Psychological Predicates" (1967): pain cannot be one particular state of the brain, as an octopus presumably feels pain with a brain built very differently, so pain has to be a functional state that many kinds of matter can realise. 6/30 starts instead from an observation within humans: we have somewhat the same kind of experience although our brains all differ, and slow-growing gliomas show living neural tissue taking over from living neural tissue. In that bio-to-bio sense (weak multiple realisability) it is evidenced; whether a different kind of substrate can take over is still open.

Also: multiple realizability, weak multiple realisability

Discussed in 6/30 Computational Vs. Physical Substrate Independence, 9/30 Weak and Strong Multiple Realisability, 10/30 I Care Deeply about the Continuity of the Human Brain, 11/30 Does My NCC Look Big in This Substrate?

Medium independence

Gualtiero Piccinini's term: a computation does not care what its vehicles are made of, as long as they have the right degrees of freedom. He contrasts it with multiple realisability, via the mousetrap, which can be built in many ways but still has to catch actual mice. 6/30: classical mind uploading turned out to need medium independence for minds, while the series needs at most multiple realisability, as in the heart-lung machine, whose "lung" is a bundle of plastic fibres.

Discussed in 6/30 Computational Vs. Physical Substrate Independence, 11/30 Does My NCC Look Big in This Substrate?

Augmented cognition

The second outcome of the Ontology. 7/30 uses it neither in DARPA's sense (a programme from the early 2000s about computers that adapt to the measured cognitive state of their user) nor in Engelbart's (1962) sense of better tools around a person, but for expanding the normal baseline of human cognition, and of conscious experience, which the name does not say. It is the bet that if a mind sits on a carrier that we built, the phenomenal space itself becomes steerable, not only the tools around it. Whatever takes what somebody can experience or do outside the range that they had before is called augmentation. In the Ontology's order it comes late, after preservation.

Also: augmentation

Discussed in 5/30 Transcending Biology, but not Physics, 6/30 Computational Vs. Physical Substrate Independence, 7/30 Augmented Cognition Needs a Baseline First

Baseline first

7/30: not to start from an augmented state as step one. First the same constraints, then changes to them, one at a time, and never both at once, with augmentation last. The baseline means the person's own, so how they experienced things and what they could do before, and no norm of what a human should be. It only shows what changed: that it is still the same someone hangs on the process having gone on, not on anybody's report. The baseline does not have to be better; it only has to come first.

Also: the same baseline

Discussed in 7/30 Augmented Cognition Needs a Baseline First, 8/30 Who Says Individuality Will Stay Forever?

Comparison and verification problem

7/30's main objection to classical mind uploading and whole brain emulation: scanned, emulated and switched on, the substrate, the body, the surroundings and probably the capacities all change in one step. Whoever comes out has no before of their own to compare to, and from in there a loss looks exactly like a difference. An emulation saying that it is fine shows nothing, as it would say that either way.

Discussed in 7/30 Augmented Cognition Needs a Baseline First

Transformative experience

L. A. Paul's name for experiences, such as becoming a vampire or having a child, that one cannot judge from the outside beforehand. 7/30 uses it for the second reason to keep the baseline first: deciding for a new sense from the outside is like ordering from a menu in a language one does not speak, where the dish then also changes what one likes to eat. So if there is a sane place to decide such things from, it is a stable one, one step at a time.

Discussed in 7/30 Augmented Cognition Needs a Baseline First

Experience machine

Robert Nozick's thought experiment. 7/30 links it to what people outside the field most often say about "being embodied in a computer simulation": that they would feel trapped. Most of them do not want new colours, but to stick around in their familiar environment, with their own place and their friends.

Discussed in 7/30 Augmented Cognition Needs a Baseline First

Collective mind

The third and last outcome of the Ontology (8/30). As soon as synthetic consciousness has been achieved via hybrid mind uploading, there is technically the possibility to merge into one giant collective mind, with one process world-line instead of "society", and phenomenally conscious as a whole, not only smart as a whole the way a society or the internet can be. Most transhumanists assume that they stay single individuals. 8/30 expects it to start occasionally, to show something instead of telling it, until coming apart again is the exception. Whether one would then still be around as oneself is left open.

Also: hivemind

Discussed in 5/30 Transcending Biology, but not Physics, 6/30 Computational Vs. Physical Substrate Independence, 8/30 Who Says Individuality Will Stay Forever?, 11/30 Does My NCC Look Big in This Substrate?, 12/30 Distributed, yet Unified

Neurophenomenal structuralism

Holger Lyre's view, which 8/30 takes seriously: what an experience is like is fixed by its position in a structure of relations to all other experiences (the Q-structure), and how that position gets activated, by the eyes or by a memory, should not matter. That it obviously does matter, as a remembered sunset is pale next to the one in front of you, is what the January 2026 essay explains with ecphoric reconstruction.

Also: Q-structure

Discussed in 5/30 Transcending Biology, but not Physics, 8/30 Who Says Individuality Will Stay Forever?

Ecphoric reconstruction

The name 8/30 uses for how a biological brain remembers: it keeps a compressed, lossy version of an experience (its engram) and rebuilds the rest from priors at recall. So remembering lands somewhere near the original position in the structure of experiences and never exactly on it. Take that bottleneck away on a substrate built without the compression constraint, and a memory would be the full experience again (lossless Q-storage), if that can be built at all.

Discussed in 8/30 Who Says Individuality Will Stay Forever?

Q-time and P-time

Two layers of time from the unpublished January 2026 essay. Q-time is personal, phenomenal time; P-time is substrate time, the physical time in which the substrate keeps moving forward like everything else. With a past that can be re-entered at full resolution, personal time turns from something one gets pushed through into something one can walk around in, like a DVD that can be rewound while the player does not get a day younger. The destruction of the forward arrow only ever happens in Q-time, and no dualism is implied: both stay within physics.

Also: substrate time

Discussed in 8/30 Who Says Individuality Will Stay Forever?

Branching identity

If one can go back into a past experiential state, one can also decide differently from there and see where it goes, so the history of what one has experienced stops being a simple line and turns into a graph with branches (a directed acyclic graph, a picture borrowed from Toby Ord). In 8/30 this is the intrapersonal, Q-time kind of hivemind: one substrate, one ongoing process, many of "me", and in P-time still exactly one person.

Also: directed acyclic graph of selves

Discussed in 8/30 Who Says Individuality Will Stay Forever?

Distributed unification

An operational requirement of the Ontology: many nodes, one mind. A brain that gets replaced piece by piece is for a long time a mix of old tissue and new parts, and that mix has to be one mind the whole time and not two. If that can be done between the parts of one brain, 8/30 sees nothing in the method that would care whose skull the parts came from, which is why collective mind is an outcome and not a goal. 12/30 calls it the fourth and last operational requirement, with the Ontology's two lines "small node count unified" (the weak form) and "large-scale, graceful degradation" (the strong one), and notes that every brain is already distributed, yet unified.

Also: many nodes, one mind

Discussed in 8/30 Who Says Individuality Will Stay Forever?, 9/30 Weak and Strong Multiple Realisability, 12/30 Distributed, yet Unified

Show, don't tell

How someone multiplies 17 × 24 in their head can at best be described, and even a much better description fails to carry over what it is like (Feynman and Tukey both "counted", in different ways, and only found out by trying). 8/30 connects this with Wittgenstein: what can be shown cannot be said, and the beetle in the box. Whatever turns experience into signals and back is still telling, only with more channels; the moment something really gets shown, there is a bit less of "two heads" than before.

Discussed in 8/30 Who Says Individuality Will Stay Forever?

Strong multiple realisability

The strong form of the Ontology's first requirement (9/30). Weak multiple realisability holds if engrams or functions get shifted around from living tissue to living tissue inside one brain, which the serial lesion effect and slowly growing low-grade gliomas show to some degree. The strong form holds if de novo tissue, tissue that was never part of that brain and maybe is not even biological, can also store and implement the same functions. If only living de novo tissue can, hybrid mind uploading has to go towards extreme biology.

Operational and axiomatic requirements

A requirement is something that has to be true about brains for hybrid mind uploading to have a chance at all. The Ontology has four of each kind. The operational ones (multiple realisability, continuer migration, morphological independence, distributed unification) are things a brain turns out to be able to do or not, so an experiment can come back with a no, and each has a weak and a strong form. The axiomatic ones (brain-centredness, methodological physicalism, non-organicism, scale separation) have to be granted before any of those experiments means anything, so each is taken in its least committal form.

Also: requirement

Lesion momentum

The clinic's word for how fast a lesion grows, the variable that the gradual reorganisation principle hangs on. In rats ten days between two stages is too fast for a frontal cortex and twenty is enough; in people with low-grade gliomas about 4 mm a year is slow enough for speech and movement. In between there is apparently nothing, and 9/30 wants it as an actual measurement, a speed limit more accurate than "slower than a stroke".

Grandmother's face problem

Slow lesions spare what is measurable from the outside, such as speech and motor function, which are retrieved all day long. But what about engrams that you are not aware of having until you recall them, such as the rarely retrieved memory of your grandmother's face? The only test for an engram is to retrieve it, and retrieving is an intervention that rehearses the trace and opens it up to change, so there is no baseline for the unmeasured ones, and forgetting does not feel like anything.

Continuer migration

The second requirement of the Ontology (10/30). It is about how the move has to go so that it is still you who arrives, and its one particular word, the continuer, brings the entire philosophy of personal identity along. 10/30 sets the thought experiments aside: whether psychological or physical continuity carries you through, what matters is the continuity of the human brain, kept running so that no accident or disease robs you of it.

Morphological independence

The third operational requirement of the Ontology (11/30): a mind tolerates local change and it tolerates major refactorisations, and size is the simplest refactorisation. Who says that a mechanistic neuron is literally the same size as the biological neuron that it replaces? On the coarse-grained view of 11/30, a delay is a length divided by a speed, so how big a replacement piece gets is decided by how slow its carriers are, with noise as the floor. Which parts of the NCC are like mass and which like radius is open.

Computational irreducibility

Wolfram's term for processes whose outcome cannot be had any faster than by letting them run. 11/30 asks which parts of what the biological brain physically does can be shortcut into something else in a different substrate while still doing or harnessing physics: another route through physics to the same place, like a car train through a tunnel, not a simulation, like moving the car on a GPS map.

Neural correlate of consciousness

11/30 asks whether the NCC is physically bound to its size. Like the Sun swapped for a black hole of the same mass, which would keep the Earth on its orbit with a radius of about 3 km instead of 700000 km, some properties may have to be there in full (candidates: the delays and what is connected to what) and others may shrink (the lengths, given a slow enough carrier).

Barrow scale

Barrow ranked civilisations by how small they can build, from things of their own size via genes, molecules and atoms to nuclei, particles and space-time itself, as the inward counterpart of Kardashev's scale of how much energy they use. 11/30 asks how far a brain could go down it, and guesses that a smaller and lighter carrier might be what allows solving death at larger scales.

Epoché

Husserl's bracket: no claims about brains or the world yet, only what's given to you as a conscious observer. In 12/30 it shows that whatever you experience comes together with the rest, as parts of one stream. It only gives how it seems, though, which can't tell how the thing is made, and it has to come off the moment somebody takes a scalpel to a brain.

Living present

Husserl's name for the width of the now: the just-past is still held in it and the about-to-come is already expected. 12/30 takes this synthesis of inner time as the most basic form of a unity that consciousness does, and notes that pulling the parts of a brain apart sooner or later turns "how far apart" into "how wide is the now".

Binding problem

Why a pack of membrane-bound neurons isn't a "micro-experiential zombie", mind-dust with nobody home. David Pearce states it as a problem for physics and answers it with quantum coherence. 12/30 takes the problem as stated without following him there.

Microconsciousness

Semir Zeki's conclusion from colour being perceived about 80 ms before motion: visual consciousness consists of many microconsciousnesses spread over space and time. In 12/30 it is part of the case that the unity Husserl describes is manufactured, out of parts that are centimetres and tens of milliseconds apart.

Integrated information theory (IIT)

In 12/30, the one theory with an explicit criterion for when two things are one. Tononi and Koch expect one mind to fall apart into two quite abruptly as the corpus callosum is weakened step by step, and the opposite for a strong enough link between two brains. A part that's only simulated doesn't count for them, because a simulation has no causal power of its own.

Virtual long-range axons

The links that carry signals between the parts of a distributed brain, a term from the synconetics essay "Death is an Engineering Challenge". 12/30 points to work that already goes in this direction, such as living axon tracts grown in a hydrogel column and a neuromorphic chip that reconnected two cut-apart cultures, and notes that a virtual axon is only adequate where all that crosses is spikes and their timing.

Ephaptic coupling

Neurons influencing each other through the electric field rather than through synapses. In 12/30 it is a reason why a cut brain cannot always be bridged by virtual axons: in slices of hippocampus, slow waves keep running across a complete cut with nothing but the field carrying them over.

China brain

Ned Block's thought experiment in which people with two-way radios each "play" one neuron. 12/30 runs a modified version twice: in version one, China carries the mail across one cut tract, so the synapses on the other side still get actual spikes at the right times; in version two, China does the sums for a part that was taken out.

Braid

Proposed in 12/30 as an alternative picture to the process world-line, which seems to imply that the process is in one place at each moment. In a braid every node is a strand, held together by the signals on their way between them, and it stays one braid although no single strand runs through all of it. Three rules keep it from coming apart: join before leave, no spares, no fission.

Also: process braid

Galántai's scale

Zoltan Galántai's ranking of civilisations by the size of the disaster that they can't survive, from a local one upwards. 11/30 finds it closest to a scale for solving death; in 12/30 you climb it by spreading out further, at the price of being slower to react as one mind.
