Glossary

Terms as they are used in Meditations on Synthetic Consciousness and Hybrid Mind Uploading by Daniel Burger. These are working definitions and will change as the series does.

Synthetic consciousness

The vision of this series: consciousness that no longer has to end, starting with the consciousness that exists now. Summarised in 0/30 as "engineered phenomenology on a designed substrate you could gradually become". "Synthetic" says that the result is the real kind of consciousness, reached via a creation process; the series calls a consciousness synthetic once it is sustained by created and engineered parts. It is used differently from "artificial consciousness" and "machine consciousness", which 1/30 discusses.

Discussed in 0/30 The Start, 1/30 Causa Finalis, 2/30 What Could Be Hybrid about Mind Uploading?

Hybrid mind uploading (HMU)

The mission of this series and the route to synthetic consciousness. Rather than creating something external to the brain that recreates the brain, it builds something that can be integrated into the brain itself. "Hybrid" means that biological and engineered parts carry one and the same process at the same time: what is hybrid is the thing that carries consciousness, never the consciousness itself. It works by non-destructive co-running and gradual replacement, and is distinguished from classical mind uploading, which treats a copy as survival.

Also: HMU

Discussed in 0/30 The Start, 1/30 Causa Finalis, 2/30 What Could Be Hybrid about Mind Uploading?

Classical mind uploading (CMU)

The name this series uses for mind uploading via whole brain emulation: scan a brain in as much detail as possible, store it, and then simulate it in order to emulate consciousness on a different substrate, a computer. Some versions are fully destructive. The series does not treat a separate instance reconstructed this way as the survival of the original consciousness.

Also: CMU

Discussed in 0/30 The Start, 1/30 Causa Finalis, 2/30 What Could Be Hybrid about Mind Uploading?

Mind uploading

The broader idea whose original goals were solving death, mind augmentation and enabling a new state of humanity, popularised in the Hans Moravec lineage. The series keeps the term for what it promises and drops its old method, splitting it into classical and hybrid mind uploading.

Discussed in 2/30 What Could Be Hybrid about Mind Uploading?

Whole brain emulation (WBE)

The academic name for the scan-and-simulate approach to mind uploading, which 2/30 traces to Randal Koene around 1998. In this series, mind uploading via whole brain emulation is what is called classical mind uploading.

Also: WBE

Discussed in 2/30 What Could Be Hybrid about Mind Uploading?

Process world-line

The 4D trajectory of the physical-dynamical and organised bounded processes going on in your head that constitute what we call your brain. It is a physical claim about the process that does the carrying, not a report of how continuous it feels, since a copy would feel continuous too. In this series, death is the irreversible end of the process world-line.

Also: world-line

Discussed in 0/30 The Start, 1/30 Causa Finalis, 2/30 What Could Be Hybrid about Mind Uploading?

Gradual brain replacement

Swapping the brain's substrate out piece by piece without destroying the physical-dynamical and organised bounded processes, ideally while adding repairability and fault tolerance, so that the process world-line hardens and becomes more resilient. It is the replacement side of hybrid mind uploading.

Also: gradual replacement

Discussed in 0/30 The Start

Mechanistic gradual brain replacement (MGBR)

The open question of whether a different, engineered substrate can take over brain function piece by piece. The serial lesion effect and the gradual reorganisation principle show that another piece of brain can take over a function; they do not show that a different substrate can. The series treats this as a hypothesis to be tested, not a result.

Also: MGBR

Discussed in 0/30 The Start

Serial lesion effect

Cited in 0/30, together with the gradual reorganisation principle, as evidence that a different piece of brain can take over a function. It does not by itself show that a different substrate can take over.

Also: SLE

Discussed in 0/30 The Start

Gradual reorganisation principle

Barrett's (2013) principle, cited in 0/30 alongside the serial lesion effect as evidence that a different piece of brain can take over a function. Like the serial lesion effect, it does not by itself show that a different substrate can take over.

Also: gradual reorganization principle, GRP

Discussed in 0/30 The Start

Cortical reorganisation

Changes in which parts of the cortex carry which functions, also called cortical remapping. Listed here as a topic related to the serial lesion effect and the gradual reorganisation principle; the series has not discussed it yet.

Also: cortical reorganization, cortical remapping
