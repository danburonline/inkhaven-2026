Augmented Cognition Needs a Baseline First

Daniel Burger·18 November 2026·1,821 words

TL;DR

Augmented cognition is the second outcome of my Ontology, but not step one: a steerable substrate should first carry the same baseline, so any change can be noticed and compared. Augmentation, like seeing a new colour, comes later, by choice, one change at a time.

The second outcome (type "Outcome") of my Ontology is called "augmented cognition" (yesterday's was "substrate independence"). This term is not very clean, as depending on who you ask, it gives you a different response. If you ask DARPA, it's a programme from the early 2000s about computers that adapt to the measured cognitive state of their user, and if you ask Engelbart (1962), it's about better tools around a person. I'm referring to neither. How I'm using it, as an outcome of hybrid mind uploading towards the vision of synthetic consciousness, is to expand the normal baseline of human cognition as we're used to it today (and of conscious experience (which the name doesn't say)). At the core, it's the bet that if a mind sits "on a carrier" that we built (physical substrate independence => in yesterday's words), then we'd be able to steer the phenomenal space itself and not only the tools around it, or as I put it in September: a substrate we can build is a substrate we can steer ([Sep 2026]). So whatever takes what somebody can experience or do outside the range that they had before, I'll call augmentation. The full catalogue I'll leave to the transhumanists. You know the usual examples: more experienced time per physical second, feelings that nobody has had yet, or colours that don't exist in the spectrum. (I'm unsure how far the first one goes btw, as a mind on such a "mechanistically created" carrier is still bound to the same physics, and to a world that keeps running at its own pace). And about those colours ... well, we all see one of them already: it's magenta! There's no single wavelength for it anywhere in the physical light spectrum, and our brain just makes it up in our phenomenal space anyway (out of red and blue light with the green missing; this is also the reason why my personal brand's main colour is magenta btw!). So if ordinary biology can already do that, then on a steerable carrier we can probably engineer – and therefore experience – a LOT more colours that don't exist in the spectrum. In 2009, adult monkeys that had been colour-blind since birth got a third cone pigment by gene therapy, and started to tell colours apart that they couldn't before ([nature.com]). Similarly, last year a handful of people were shown a colour outside the natural gamut, by a laser that stimulated (almost) only their M cones (they called it "olo": [science.org]). And in the same vein, I suspect that some of what gets called augmentation is possible in biology already, so what a steerable substrate would add is range, and hopefully a way back.

I'll say up front that I think this is one of the most exciting things that the whole endeavour of the synthetic consciousness vision makes possible. However, what I'm more careful about is to not start from an augmented state as step one. It's tempting to skip the boring part, and for parts of the transhumanism that I come from the augmented state is the whole point, but it's not what most people that I talk to are after. In the Ontology's order it looks like this btw => understanding consciousness is what makes experience steerable and checkable, transcending humanity is what would use that, and so this outcome comes late. That augmentation comes after preservation I've said before ([Sep 2026]). What I haven't really said so far is WHY. So I sat down to write it out for today's meditation, and going by my notes, I end up with three reasons:

– Number one is the one that I actually care about the most (the other two are more like side arguments, with all due respect to them), and it's just a simple question: how would you even notice? Say my first state on the new carrier is already an augmented one. One day I wake up in there and look at a wide sky with a few colours in it that I've never seen. I find some things sharper than before, and some oddly flat. And you ask me, why do you think that is? I'd guess the new colours, or the new pace of time. I'd come up with plenty of clever explanations, and I couldn't check a single one of them. And the answer might just be that before I woke up, something of mine went missing on the way (from in there, a loss looks exactly like a difference!). Suppose that instead I come back as the same me into the same place. Sitting in my own kitchen with the same coffee as always, I'd expect it to taste the way it always did. I might not catch the first thing that's off, but after a few of them (a taste, a word that's gone, a face, the way to the bakery, my own handwriting … I don't know yet how many it takes) I could tell pretty well what went missing, and later what got added. A careful reader of yesterday's meditation (I hope that there are a few) would look at this and say, "That's just your outcome read backwards => the point there was to re-create the SAME constraints, and augmenting means changing them, so first the same ones, then changes to them, and never both at once." And that's my comparison and verification problem with classical mind uploading and whole brain emulation (ha, I know, "but the emulation says that it's fine". Sure? It would say that either way!). Run it that way: you get scanned, you get emulated/simulated, and switched on. Result: the substrate changed, the body changed, the surroundings changed and prob the capacities too, all in that one step. Checklist in hand, somebody watches whoever came out, and asks how it feels in there. They conclude that everything is approximately fine. At this point I'm somewhat at a loss for where to even start comparing, as it's immediately such an alien state to transition to (in silicon). Consider the one who came out and says, "Eh, feels like me, I guess?" I ask: compared to what? Those words come easily enough, but there's no before of their own to say them from, as the original has either stopped or is somebody else by now (and yes, put the emulation into a robot in the very same environment, and it still has no "before"). I don't want to oversell the baseline tho, as it only shows what changed. One could come back to the same kitchen, find the same coffee, and think that this proves that it's still the same someone. Not so. That hangs on the process having gone on (the hypothesis from day four), and on nobody's report, mine included. "Status-quo bias!" you cry. "You just like things the way they are!" Maybe I do, but the baseline doesn't have to be better for any of this; it only has to come first.

– The second reason is about choosing, and for this one I had help, as the philosopher L. A. Paul has a name for such things => transformative experiences (her own examples are becoming a vampire and having a child, afaik). To me, deciding for a new sense from the outside sounds like someone ordering in a restaurant where the menu is in a language that they don't speak, and where the dish then also changes what they like to eat. Or if you had the ability to switch to a different pace of time, do you imagine that you could say beforehand if you'd ever want to switch back? I couldn't. So if there's a sane place to decide such things from, then it's a stable one, and one step at a time. I do believe that even the most old-school person, who'd want to stay as natural as possible in their extended lifespan, would at some point want to try what a mechanistic brain substrate can do (hence augmenting their cognitive functions and consciousness).

– The third reason is the weakest as an argument, and the one that I hear the most. I've talked to a bunch of people about this by now, also people far outside the field, and most of them have a hard time with "being embodied in a computer simulation". What I hear most is that they'd feel trapped, which philosophers know from Nozick's experience machine, and which I've wondered about from the other side => at which point does a brain stop accepting a simulation as its lived reality ([Oct 2025])? They don't want a hundred new colours, or to merge with other people (the third outcome of the Ontology btw, which we will discuss tomorrow!). They simply want to stick around in their familiar environment. It's a lot easier to tell somebody that we take the brain out of the failing body while they stay alive (the stop-dying part), replace it gradually and mechanistically (the solving-death part), and that they then come back to their own place and their friends.

Take somebody like my mother. If she ever had this option, I don't think that she'd want a digital utopia, with the vastness of simulated worlds and new augmented experiences waiting for her. She would (at least initially) want to come back, be in her lovely garden, play with her dogs and have a normal life, just a bit longer and way healthier than before. By baseline I mean hers btw, so how she experienced things and what she could do before, and no norm of what a human should be.

PS: One correction to my own picture tho, as she wouldn't come back in the body that she left. I've said before that I wouldn't put a brain back into something that can get hit by a bus on the way out of the hospital, and that it should stay in a secure, fault-tolerant facility ([Jul 2026]), so the garden would be reached through a body that's steered from a distance, by a brain that's still hers (the same 4D process world line). That body is new, with other hands and other senses, and it has to become hers first. So even the most conservative version of this has changes in it, which is exactly why I want them one at a time, and augmentation last. It's not a main goal then (survival is: [Jul 2026], at least initially as goal one in the Ontology), and it needs a lot of work on what it means to still be human and on what exactly can be engineered, with so many question marks that I see it as a Pandora's box of issues, and of opportunities. But whatever it is, we don't want to deal with it at the start. At the start, we should have the same baseline to compare to, with the option of more, and that's probably the essence of today's meditation.
